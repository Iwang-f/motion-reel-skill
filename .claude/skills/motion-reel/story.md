# Story: how a reel earns its minute

Read before the script. Motion holds attention; the story decides whether there is anything worth holding it for. The
timings below are measured on other channels' reels (`REFERENCES/motion/reels-study-2026-10.md`, `reels-study-2026-11.md`); the
psychology is craft, not measured on our films. What our own films earned is in `docs/results.md`: read it before choosing a hook,
and it outranks all of this.

A viewer decides three times: **stay** (0–3 s), **keep watching** (every few seconds), **act** (the end). A figure on black
answers none of them. Build for those three decisions and let the motion serve them.

The picture has its own study: `REFERENCES/motion/visual-grammar-2026-10.md`. Story decides what to say; that file decides
how it looks, and neither replaces the other.

## Topic

Settle the subject before the spine: with the same channel, narrator and format, the subject moved views about 60× (How Come?,
25 shorts). When the brief fixes the subject, apply this to the angle. Take the highest rung that stays true:

| Rung | The viewer… | How Come? median |
|---|---|---|
| **Underfoot** | stands on it, passes it, obeys it most days: a floor, a roof, a railway crossing | 4.2M (n=8) |
| **In sight** | owns it or has seen it, rarely thinks about it: a fridge, a crane, a power line | 1.4M (n=14) |
| **Inside** | cannot see it: a chip, binary, photons | 0.15M (n=3) |

- **Oddity:** one thing the viewer has noticed and never had explained, sayable as a title: `Kenapa` + the thing + the odd part.
  The title form alone carries nothing (a stove-flame short used it, 0.28M); the oddity has to come from the viewer's own life.
- **Whose day:** the first sentence is about the viewer's day. Two films with one formula, five days apart, split 126× on this.

For OpenView (a hypothesis, untested on our films): underfoot is what the viewer already does in their own broker app, a stock they
hold, a dividend that arrived, a price that jumped. Inside is registers, legal definitions and graph structure. Start up there and
carry the viewer down.

## The viewer

The viewer is the **investigator** (owner, 5 Nov 2026: "lebih interaktif gitu dengan audience"). Kamu opens the filing, flips the tag,
guesses, hits the dead end, and the narrator talks to that one person, in the present tense. Every winner in the study speaks in the
second person. Of our last six films only ANDA does (`docs/retro-2026-11-07.md`); the others read as a report about other people.

| Move | What it does | From our films |
|---|---|---|
| **Action** | Kamu does the looking; the fact is what they find | `Prospektus mencatat enam pemegang. Yuk, kita balik tagnya.` (ANDA) |
| **Prediction** | Stop before the answer and make them guess | `Namanya beda. Menurut kamu, pemilik manfaatnya beda juga?` → `Balik tagnya. Yang tercatat sama: Ivan Victor.` (ANDA) |
| **Ritual** | One physical action the film repeats, theirs to do | `Balik tagnya` at l04 and l09 (ANDA) |
| **Callback** | Pays back those who stayed | `Ingat Arkara?` (ANDA) |
| **Own screen** | The thing already in their hand | `Aplikasi brokermu menampilkan satu harga. Catatan ini menunjukkan tiga.` (DOOH) |

- **Sources as moves.** Where the viewer could do the looking, the source is their action: `Buka keterbukaannya`. A second-hand
  source lives on the plate and in the caption, and is spoken only when legal asks, after the first line. Either way it stays true as
  heard (Truth).
- **Names they know.** Translate a register name into what the viewer already knows: on TMPO the owner pushed Holywings, Akademi Crypto
  and FLOQ forward over the PT names. PT names go on objects.
- **Load.** A stranger holds about four new names and figures, then needs a line with nothing new. Alike names cost double: ANDA's cold
  reader left at `Bhumi Caraka Persada`, 15 s after `Bumi Artha Caraka`. Say the name again wherever `Dia` or `Di atasnya` could point at
  two things: ANDA, BYAN and TMPO each lost a reader there. `tools/script.mjs` warns where a script is dense; the Cold read's LOST finds
  where it confuses.
- **Plain words.** Say it as to a friend. A legal term the film needs (`pemilik manfaat`) comes once with its gloss, sourced like any
  definition, and the gloss carries on from there. `tercatat` five times, as on TMPO, is a filing speaking; the last three cold readers
  named both words.

## The spine

Write this table before the script, one line a row, in `<shotlist>`. A row you cannot fill is the finding: go back to
the idea, not to the animation.

| Beat | Where | Its job | Measured in the reels |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stake** | 0–3 s | Name a person or a situation the viewer recognises, and what is wrong with it. The voice starts on frame 0 and the picture shows the same thing, already in motion | A throbbing toe under a blanket; a claim that flips a belief; a tale in one sentence |
| **Loop** | 3–6 s | One question the film promises to close | Question at 4–5 s, then the series' ritual line at 5–6 s, all three scandulu reels |
| **Build** | to 45 % | Concrete steps, one idea per 3–4 s, each a thing you could point at. Abstract becomes physical | Immune cells are guards with ID cards; a story of coins going 10, 5, 1 |
| **Turn** | 40–60 % | What flips the viewer's belief. It opens a second loop, because attention sags here | "Dan bagian paling ngeri" 40–61 %; "Kenapa bisa salah baca?" 50 %; "bukan cuma jarumnya" 55 % |
| **Reveal** | 60–75 % | The answer, reworded in one sentence. On OpenView films the product delivers it | "Jadi ternyata" 64–74 %; the concept named 60 % |
| **So what** | 65–90 % | Why it matters to the viewer's own next move, ending on one line worth repeating. The last idea before the Ask | "Dan manusia juga begitu" 65–72 %; "Jadi ingat ini" 89–90 %; a tip at 85 % |
| **Ask** | last 3 s | One action, one only, that costs a thumb: a question the comments can answer | Comment prompt at 94 %, all three scandulu reels, which also feeds the next episode's hook |

Ours to match: So what `Sebelum beli saham, lihat dulu siapa yang sudah di dalam.` (Delisting), `Sebelum masuk tambang, baca papan
tagnya.` (ANDA); Ask `IPO mana lagi? Tulis di komentar.` (ANDA, which then stacked two more asks in front of it).

Each film carries one ritual and one Ask (owner, 2026-11-07). The ritual is the series' line, the same words in every episode,
a move the viewer makes (`Balik tagnya`), never a disclaimer. The Ask is the comment prompt, the last line, and nothing else asks:
the So what's check in OpenView is a reason, not a second ask. Propose the series' ritual words with the next film's spine; once
the owner OKs them, record them in `docs/content-map.md` and reuse them verbatim.

Pick the shape from the brief; each account is one shape of this spine:

- **Ladder** (70–75 s): the crudest version of the thing, already called the thing → a failure you can feel, with a joke on it →
  the fix → the fix's name → the next failure, four to six times → the title's oddity answered at 55–65 % → recognition line at
  70–86 % → tip at 89 % → the thing finished → comment. Fits how a rule, a market or an institution came to be: "you" try each
  step in turn. In the house 40 s it holds two or three rungs; ask the owner for the length if it needs more.
- **Another day** (155 s): you wake up in a world where one thing is different; each hour is a small scene and then the real number;
  the turn lands on the viewer at about 83 %; the end returns to the first frame. Fits a "what if" on a figure. In the house 40 s,
  three hours of one day.
- **Case file** (67 s): the viewer's own room is the scene; suspects with name tags, each dismissed; the least likely last. Fits a
  list that would otherwise be a ranking.
- **Tale with a sum** (32 s): one person, one small deal, two numbers checkable in the head, one line that flips it. Fits a single
  mechanism and the house length as it stands.
- **Mechanism** (50 s): symptom or myth → question → steps through one metaphor → turn → "jadi ternyata" → tip → comment. Fits
  how a chain, a rule or a market event works.
- **Parable** (65–70 s): a tale in concrete events → twist → the concept named → the human parallel → one line. Fits a principle
  ("the biggest holder is not always the controller"). The reels ran 65–70 s; within the house 40 s, keep one event chain and
  drop the concept's name, or ask the owner for the length.
- **Fable** (15 s, no VO, loops): one metaphor acted out in one continuous shot. Fits a single feeling; the loop is the point.

## Hooks

Our weakest openings start from what we know; strong ones start from what the viewer feels.

- Weak: `Rp92,57 miliar, ANDI resmi berganti pengendali.` True, and a stranger has no reason to care.
- Weak: `Sepertiga saham ANDI berpindah tangan.` Reworded, it is the same news. A fact about a company is not a stake.
- Weak: `Pemegang saham baru Bayan Resources: cuma 2 persen. Tapi nama di baliknya bukan nama biasa.` (6,8 s) The stake waits for the
  second sentence, and it withholds a name with nothing to pull on.
- Weak: `Sebulan tender, 0 saham masuk. Menurut pemberitaan yang mengutip keterbukaan.` The source clause spends the hook.
- Strong: `Namanya tak ada di daftar. Tapi Hermanto Tanoko memegang 16% saham ini.` (HELI) A known name, something missing, a
  question that writes itself.
- Strong: `Lo Kheng Hong masih tercatat memegang 1% saham Sritex.` (3,8 s) A known name in an odd place, then `10 November, saham ini
  keluar dari bursa.` `Siapa lagi yang masih di dalam?`
- Strong: `Kalau kamu ikut IPO ANDA, kamu masuk bareng siapa?` The viewer's own act, and the Loop in line one. As taken, with the full
  company name in it, this line ran 5,0 s; the name cost about 1,3 s of that.

Recipes: **the missing name** (known figure, absent from the obvious place); **the flipped belief** ("banyak yang ngira X,
padahal kebalikannya"); **the felt moment** (the viewer in the situation, in their words); **the comment reply** (a real
follower's question on screen, measured on the rain reel; it needs one of the others too, since How Come?'s solar short opened on
one and got 0.14M); **the tale** (one sentence with a character and a threat); **the crude build** (`Kalau kamu` + the crudest act +
`kamu udah punya` + the thing, first failure by 5 s); **the changed morning** (you wake up and one thing outside your house is gone);
**the case** (your own room as a crime scene); **join the game** (day one of a premise, and the viewer's part in it).

Write three first lines from three different recipes, then lead with the one whose subject is `kamu`, or a name a stranger already
knows; a register PT or a name only filings carry is neither. The first line runs 4,5 s at most, which is 63 characters: our takes speak
about 14 a second whatever the word count, so a long name spends it. The source goes on the plate. The best surprise in the story usually
belongs in the first line: look for it at the Turn and move it up.

## Attention, turned into pictures

| Mechanism | What it does | How to draw it |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | An open question is uncomfortable to leave | State the gap in the first line; keep the answer off screen until the Reveal |
| Isolation | The one different thing gets seen | One accent, on the one thing that matters: the yellow snake among grey rats, the white figure among grey crowds |
| Concreteness | A thing is remembered, a noun phrase is not | A chain you can follow, a door, a locked drawer; never a label over a box |
| Character | We track faces and intent | A small code-drawn figure with a stake. Characters stay code-drawn and their size is a per-film decision (rulings R-122, R-149) |
| Pattern break | Change restarts attention | A colour flip on the Turn: the reels' red frames land on threats, not on a timer |
| Unfinished set | A blurred list begs completion | Show three, blur the rest: "+29 perusahaan lainnya" with a lock |
| Self-reference | "This is about me" outlasts "this is interesting" | "Sebelum kamu beli…", the viewer's own screen, their own decision |
| Peak and end | The strongest moment and the last are what is remembered | The best image on the Reveal, the So what as the last idea. The legal line lives on the plate and in the caption; when it must be spoken it comes before the So what. `Data mencatat siapa, bukan kenapa` closed TMPO, Delisting and BYAN, so a disclaimer is what they left behind: it is the line TMPO's cold readers would repeat, two reads of three |
| Relay | A closed question with nothing open is where people leave | Close one question only as the next opens. Past 60 s, a new one every 20–30 s: Pandu's l11–l18 is a CV with none open |
| Repetition | A ritual turns episodes into a series | Same pivot words and same frame every episode: both Indonesian accounts reuse theirs verbatim |
| Failure | A thing that breaks is a small open loop, and it is where the joke goes | Show it failing: water dripping, a tile lifting. In an ownership film, the dead end the viewer hits: a list that stops, a PT with no face. Winners have one every 12–18 s |
| Recognition | Something seen a thousand times, finally explained, gets told to a friend | A `pantes…` or `makanya…` line over the thing as the viewer knows it, finished and ordinary |

## The product is the plot

The film earns the product by making it the only way to answer the viewer's last question. Test it twice: remove the
product and the story must still stand; remove the story and the product must still be needed.

- The product is first named at the Reveal, as an action doing the one thing the story needs. A feature list has no place in a reel.
- Until the Reveal, kamu acts with what they already hold: a guess, their broker app, the filing. A rerun of ANDI opened with `Di OpenView,
  klik PT-nya` at 33 %, and its cold reader left there: `mendadak terdengar seperti iklan aplikasi`. `tools/script.mjs` warns on it.
- End on what the viewer still wants to know, with the product holding it: the locked list.
- The So what's last line says what the viewer checks there and what they get: `Sebelum ikut IPO berikutnya, cek dulu siapa di baliknya di
  OpenView.` The Ask comes after it and stays one action. No cold read of six on `Telusuri … di OpenView` was told; BYAN's said `Saya tidak
  tahu apa itu OpenView`, ANDA's `baru dijanjikan, belum ditunjukkan`.
- Choose the topic and the formula from `docs/content-map.md`.

## Truth

Attention never outranks accuracy. These are claims about real people and companies.

- The antagonist is not knowing, never a person. Data, not accusation: a Turn may surprise, it may not imply wrongdoing.
- Every name and figure comes from a source already in `references/sources.md`. What the app does not show stays out.
- **True as heard.** A viewer hears the sentence and never sees the footnote, so each sentence is true as a listener takes it. BYAN's
  `Hari itu, Jhonlin membeli persis sebanyak itu` is exact per filing and false per day, and that surfaced after the takes were paid.
  A listener does the sums too (ANDA's 35 and 60 beside `70 persen sisanya` read as wrong) and takes order as a claim: two true sentences
  side by side say the two connect, and a disclaimer after them leaves that standing. BYAN's cold reader heard `bukan kenapa` and
  still asked `apakah ini kebetulan atau terkoordinasi`; another quoted the denial and said the order `sudah menanam kesan itu`.
- Promotional films name no register and carry no "sumber" line (R-120); the citation goes in the post caption, as the
  mindwander reels do. Investigative films follow their brief.
- The product claims only what `PRODUCT.md` allows: no testimonials, pricing, case studies or coverage figures that do not exist.

## Captions

Measured: one line of 3–4 words with the spoken word in the accent (scandulu), or one word at a time mid-frame (mindwander).
Ours run two lines, left-aligned. Default to one centred line: `buildCaptions` with `maxLines: 1`. On the November accounts captions
sit at 65–70 % of the frame height, y 1250–1350 at 1920, above our default band of 1420–1600 (`tools/layout.mjs`), and clear of the
feed's own text; How Come? builds the phrase word by word, in white, with no accent.

## Before the script leaves this step

Write `<project>/brief/script-review.md`: the `script: <hash>` line that `node tools/script.mjs --film=<film>` prints, then the three
sections below. Run that tool until it passes; `tools/vo.mjs` spends no credit on a new script before it does. A check that a line of the
owner's cannot meet takes a waiver with the owner's words (`scriptWaivers` in `vo-lines.json`).

**`## Tests`.** Each quotes its line from the script. A test with no line to quote has failed.

- **Rung:** the subject or angle sits on the highest rung of Topic that stays true.
- **Investigator:** the line where kamu acts, and the line where kamu predicts or is asked.
- **Failure:** Ladder and Mechanism scripts have one felt failure every 12–18 s (a 40 s film has at least two).
- **Recognition:** one line sends the viewer back to something they have seen themselves.
- **Relay:** the question open at each third of the film.
- **So what:** the last idea before the Ask, worth repeating, saying what the viewer checks in the product; then the one Ask.
- **Retell:** the film fits in one sentence a viewer would say to a friend. That sentence is the brief's `INTI`.

**`## True as heard`.** One row for every sentence carrying a name, figure, date or relation: the sentence, its source in
`references/sources.md`, what a listener takes it to mean, and whether that is true. The last row is the whole film: what it leaves a
listener believing, against `brief/decisions.md`.

**`## Cold read`.** `node tools/script.mjs --film=<film> --cold-read` prints the reader's prompt and the script. Give that text alone to
a fresh-context subagent (`Agent`, `model: sonnet`) and paste its answers here. It passes when:

- YOUR PART quotes a line before the Ask;
- QUESTION opens and closes;
- SURPRISE and REPEAT quote something other than a disclaimer;
- LAST names the So what in the reader's own words;
- SUSPECT states nothing `brief/decisions.md` rules out. You make this comparison, since the reader has no brief.

SCROLL, LOST and REPORT VOICE are the fix list. The reader has no picture, so a line the picture carries can read as lost (`Sebelum masuk
tambang, baca papan tagnya`, ANDA): check it against the shot first. The quoted line moves between readers (TMPO's REPEAT: l21, l02, l21)
while the kind of fault holds, so fix the kind. Fix what the read names and read again with a new reader; what the second read still
misses goes to the owner with the spine. A Cold read finds faults; it predicts no views.
